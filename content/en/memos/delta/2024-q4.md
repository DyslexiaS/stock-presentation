**At a glance.** A soft quarter inside a solid year, and the call where Delta stated an ambition few Taiwan suppliers can: to move **above the rack** into medium-voltage DC and solid-state transformers, extending from power supplier toward the grid architecture itself.

## What changed this quarter

**Margin broke its run.** Gross margin fell from 34.9% in Q3 to **30.8%**, and net income dropped 42% sequentially. Management gave three causes: an unfavourable mix while AI server products crossed generations, larger inventory write-downs, and a one-off quality problem at the Thailand plant.

**The ambition moved up the stack.** Asked about medium-voltage DC for data centers, management said it is actively developing **MV inverters and solid-state transformers**, and wants to extend from being a power supply vendor into the grid architecture. That is the layer NVIDIA would later specify for converting 13.8–35 kV AC directly to 800 VDC.

**The rack roadmap was spelled out.** Management described power moving from a single **PSU**, to an integrated **power shelf**, to a rack-level **sidecar or power train** — with GPU power rising from hundreds of watts past 1,500 W. Each step raises Delta's content per rack.

Operating expense rose 17% on R&D, acquisition-related amortisation, and litigation. The proposed dividend was **NT$7.00**, a payout slightly above 50%.

## NVIDIA 800V read-through

This call predates NVIDIA's whitepaper, but it contains the two things that matter for Delta's later inclusion on the official partner list.

**Medium-voltage DC and SST.** Delta named both as active development areas. In NVIDIA's eventual reference design, a 17.5 MW block is built from 3.5 MW MV rectifiers, with SSTs as the future-proof alternative at up to 7.5 MVA. Delta is the only Taiwan name that has claimed a seat at that layer.

**The PSU-to-rack progression.** Delta described exactly the centralisation NVIDIA later formalised, and noted that **DC-DC converters gain importance** in the new architecture — the stage NVIDIA assigns to a 64:1 LLC converter beside the GPU.

Delta said it would show next-generation Blackwell power and cooling at GTC.

## Segments that moved

**Power electronics** was 52% of Q4 revenue. AI server products were the growth driver but were between generations, which is part of why mix hurt.

**Infrastructure** was 27% and grew **21% year on year** on data-center power and cooling — the segment where liquid cooling sits.

**Automation** was 12% and returned to growth, up 7% year on year, with a modest recovery expected through 2025.

**Mobility** was 9% and flat for the year on soft EV demand, but January 2025 revenue alone approached the entire prior-year run rate, which management read as a genuine recovery rather than noise.

## Guidance

- **2025 revenue growth to accelerate**, led by AI servers and EV.
- **Gross margin** expected to be stable versus 2024, helped by mix.
- **Operating expense** growth to slow, though likely still double digit.
- **Capex** to stay high to support capacity and technology upgrades.

## Management Q&A

**Q:** What is Delta's position in medium-voltage DC products for data centres?

**A:** The facility-level medium-voltage market is still led by a few large names, and Delta has not done a great deal there yet. As a power-solutions supplier it wants to move up toward grid architecture, and it is developing products such as medium-voltage inverters and solid-state transformers. "Medium voltage" here means tens of thousands of volts, which is not the same as the 800V that data-centre racks call high voltage.

**Q:** What is the product plan for next-generation NVIDIA platforms such as GB200, GB300 and Rubin?

**A:** GPU power keeps rising — from a few hundred watts toward **1,500 W** and beyond — so PSU wattage follows. The architecture is moving from a single PSU to a Power Shelf that packs several PSUs, then to rack-level sidecar or power-train solutions, which adds more of the bill for Delta, and DC-DC converters become more important in the new stack. New Blackwell-generation power and cooling were due to be shown at GTC; Rubin is still further out and still carries some uncertainty.

**Q:** What is the outlook for the EV business?

**A:** EV was roughly **flat** in 2024 after years of 50–100% growth, on slower end-market demand. 2025 is described as highly uncertain, and management does not dare expect much growth; without growth, profitability is not easy either.

**Q:** Why did Q4 2024 gross margin fall?

**A:** Three reasons: mix, with AI-server power in a generation gap and high-margin passives in their seasonal dip; larger inventory write-down provisions as stock levels rose; and one-off costs from a product-quality issue at a Thailand plant. Q2–Q3 margins had also been flattered by inventory-provision reversals that were not as large in Q4.

**Q:** What is the 2025 capex plan?

**A:** Capex is expected to stay at a relatively high level to support capacity expansion and technology upgrades, especially in the faster-growing businesses.

**Q:** What is the dividend policy?

**A:** The board proposed a **NT$7.00** cash dividend for 2024, a payout just above **50%**, in line with the articles of incorporation, which set a floor of 50%.
