## Who Delta is

Delta Electronics (TW:2308) is Taiwan's largest power electronics company and, by product breadth, the one Taiwan name that can sell almost every block of NVIDIA's 800 VDC diagram — from the medium-voltage conversion above the rack down to the DC-DC stage beside the GPU.

It is one of four Taiwan-listed companies on **NVIDIA's published 800 VDC ecosystem list**, under power system components, alongside Lite-On, BizLink and Lead Wealth.

## Business mix

Delta reorganised into four segments from 2024, when EV powertrain was carved out of power electronics:

- **Power electronics** — roughly half of revenue. Server and AI server power supplies, passive components, cooling fans, power shelves, rack power, and DC-DC converters.
- **Infrastructure** — data-center power and cooling, UPS, telecom power, EV charging, and energy storage. This is where the liquid-cooling business sits.
- **Automation** — industrial and building automation, being repositioned from machine automation toward process and production automation.
- **Mobility** — EV powertrain, targeted at roughly 15% gross margin because autos are not a high-margin business.

## Where it sits in the NVIDIA 800 VDC stack

Two places at once, which is unusual.

Inside the rack, Delta makes the power shelf, BBU, and DC-DC. It has claimed to be one of only about two companies worldwide capable of mass-producing AI server DC-DC converters — the stage NVIDIA's architecture assigns to a 64:1 LLC converter taking 800 V straight to 12 V.

Above the rack, Delta is developing **medium-voltage inverters and solid-state transformers**, with a stated ambition to move from power supplier to grid architecture. That is the MV rectifier / SST layer NVIDIA specifies for converting 13.8–35 kV AC directly to 800 VDC.

## Strategy

R&D is held at **8% or more of revenue** and capex above **NT$20bn a year**, funding plants in Taiwan, Thailand, the US, Hungary, India, and Chongqing. Thailand and India together are around 30% of capacity; Delta has been explicit that it is not exiting China, which it expects to remain above half of capacity. Longer-term bets include solid-oxide fuel cells under a Ceres Power licence, and microgrids.
