## Who Foxconn is

Hon Hai Precision Industry (TW:2317), known globally as Foxconn, is the world's largest contract electronics manufacturer: 233 sites across 24 countries and more than **US$170bn** of annual procurement. It is not on NVIDIA's published power-component list, but foreign brokers named it among the Taiwan partners building **Kyber**, the 576-GPU rack that 800 VDC exists to feed.

## Business mix

Four product segments, with the balance shifting fast toward AI:

- **Cloud and networking** — the growth engine. AI servers, general servers, and networking. AI server revenue passed half of all server revenue during 2025.
- **Consumer electronics** — smartphones and related, historically the largest segment and still around 40%.
- **Computing** — PCs and notebooks.
- **Components and others** — the highest-margin piece, and the strategic priority.

## Where it sits in the NVIDIA 800 VDC stack

Foxconn is the integrator that physically builds the rack, but the more interesting number is how much of the rack it intends to make itself.

Management targets **50–60% component self-sufficiency** on products it assembles, chips excepted. In an AI rack, the non-GPU bill of materials is exactly what 800 VDC changes: **busbars** — the item NVIDIA says gets 200 kg lighter per rack — plus **CDUs, universal quick disconnects, manifolds, and cold plates**, all of which Foxconn has put into in-house development and customer validation. It also holds a dominant position further upstream, having claimed 100% share of GPU modules and around half of GPU baseboards.

On the silicon side, Foxconn has developed **1200 V SiC modules** for EV drivetrains and a SiC module plant in Hsinchu. NVIDIA's whitepaper credits SiC and GaN maturity, driven by the EV industry's move from 400 V to 800 V, as a precondition for 800 VDC in data centers.

For Taiwan component vendors this cuts both ways: Foxconn racks need parts, and Foxconn intends to make many of those parts itself.
