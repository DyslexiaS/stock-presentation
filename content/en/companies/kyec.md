## Who KYEC is

King Yuan Electronics (TW:2449) is a **pure-play semiconductor test** house: wafer probing, final test, burn-in, and backend support. Testing is about **98%** of sales. It is the largest dedicated test specialist globally by that cut. It does **not** run CoWoS, FOCoS or any 2.5D line. It tests the AI GPU and ASIC that come off those packages.

It is **not** on NVIDIA's published 800 VDC list. It belongs here because CoWoS-class AI parts need long, hot, high-power test loops, and because KYEC's own call dated Rubin, CSP ASIC and that longer test time.

## Business mix

From the Q4 2025 investor-update deck used on the 19 March 2026 call:

- **Final test (FT)** — **58.7%** of 2025 sales, including system-level test (SLT).
- **Chip probing (CP)** — **30.6%**.
- **Burn-in** — **7.7%**. Self-made high-power ovens are the AI-GPU claim.
- **Other** — equipment, tools, lease, miscellaneous.

Customers are mostly fabless. The growth argument on the call is mix, not a new service line: AI GPU (Blackwell into Rubin) plus CSP ASIC (Google TPU, AWS Trainium and others), with test time and ASP up as TDP and complexity rise.

## Where it sits in the NVIDIA 800 VDC stack

Downstream of the package, upstream of the rack. A CoWoS GPU still has to be probed, burned in and final-tested before an ODM can ship a cabinet. The March 2026 call never named 800 V or HVDC.

What it did say about the AI package:

- Next-generation AI chips take **more than 70%** longer to test than the prior generation. KYEC bills time.
- 2026 capex on that call was **NT$393.72bn**, described as customer-driven for **NVIDIA Rubin** and CSP-ASIC test specs the existing fleet cannot cover. A later April 2026 board raised the same budget to **NT$500bn**; that figure is not in the March note.
- AI test capacity was **full**. Further growth waits on cleanroom.

Do not read KYEC as a CoWoS foundry or as a named NVIDIA 800V vendor. It is the test slot on a CoWoS-class part.
